\textbf{Q3 (P6, P7, Q4).}\quad
\textit{What are the benchmark inputs, STBoost design, and cell-alignment
procedure?}

\textbf{A.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The benchmark predicts a cell-resolved RNA profile from histology alone;
no spot-level expression is supplied at inference. Training pairs each profile
directly with a local cell crop and a larger tissue-context crop.
\item STBoost is the cell-aligned interface that replaces a spot crop with
hierarchical cell/context inputs while retaining a method's prediction
modules. STBoosted BLEEP is published BLEEP retrained through this interface;
STBoost-Ref is our image-only reference predictor. We will define STBoost and
BLEEP at first mention and remove the ambiguous label ``Ours.''
\item Xenium uses native boundaries and transcript coordinates. For HD,
official transforms register 2-\(\mu\)m bins to CellViT contours; overlaps are
aggregated, conflicts go to the nearest centroid, and unsupported polygons are
excluded---rather than matching one spot center to one cell. In our
9,000-polygon audit, canonical-bin coverage was 49.4\%, 97.5\%, and 50.0\%
across lung, brain, and pancreas. Among supported polygons, \(\pm1\)-\(\mu\)m
shifts gave bin Jaccard 0.706--0.816 and expression cosine 0.936--0.994;
erosion/dilation was more disruptive (Jaccard 0.462--0.720; cosine
0.871--0.993; median absolute UMI change 32.8--66.6\%). We will move these
details into the main body and keep native Xenium separate from derived HD.
\end{itemize}

\questionsep
\textbf{Q4 (Q3, L3).}\quad
\textit{What does cell alignment add beyond spot-centered prediction?}

\textbf{A.}\enspace We thank the reviewer for this important suggestion.
\begin{itemize}
\item Across six native-Xenium samples, we held the pipeline fixed and changed
only target scale (cell/8/16/55 \(\mu\)m): Gene Pearson was
0.365/0.365/0.363/0.330, showing averaging can retain a similar global score.
\item We then audited two representative samples. At 55 \(\mu\)m, pseudo-spots
mixed cell types in 55.5--66.4\% of regions and affected 73.8--81.0\% of cells
in dense tissue, versus 3.5--4.1\% and 7.0--8.3\% in sparse tissue.
\item Cell alignment thus reveals heterogeneity hidden by averaging and enables
cell-resolved studies such as cell--cell communication, virtual-cell
perturbation prediction, and tracing perturbation effects through interactions
across spatial tissue.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Q5 (P4, P8, P9, L4).}\quad
\textit{What do the context analysis and available metadata support?}

\textbf{A.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Context is an orthogonal robustness axis, not evidence of single-cell
validity. Because age is nested within sample/patient and panels differ,
AK/AD/AL supports only a ``sample/age-confounded context shift.''
This motivates Average/Worst/Gap/Support reporting rather than causal
attribution.
\item The breadth benchmark uses 30 native-Xenium samples; HD transfer uses 16
source/target samples in eight pairs. Upstream donor/animal IDs are incomplete,
so these are sample-held-out unless donor independence is verified.
\item We will report per-organ sample records beside cell counts and mark
unavailable patient/animal IDs explicitly. Age, sex, and disease are retained
only when reported; ethnicity is undocumented. We will report missingness,
never infer sensitive attributes, and require multiple verified donors per
subgroup comparison.
\end{itemize}
