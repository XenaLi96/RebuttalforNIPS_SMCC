\textbf{Q3 (P6, P7).}\quad
\textit{What are the benchmark inputs, experimental design, and STBoost
definitions?}

\textbf{A.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Input/output:} histology alone predicts aligned-cell RNA; no
molecular input is used at inference. Each target has local-cell and
tissue-context crops.
\item \textbf{Protocols/metrics:} spatial holdout, cross-sample, verified
cross-patient, and cross-platform tests use fixed splits; metrics are gene/cell
Pearson/Spearman and expression-detection F1.
\item \textbf{STBoost:} hierarchical cell/context images and cell-resolved
targets replace a method's spot interface while its predictor is retained.
Table BLEEP is STBoosted BLEEP; STBoost-Ref is our image-only reference.
\item Because this ED-track submission is a dataset paper, we initially placed
the detailed STBoost method, equations, and architecture in the Appendix. This
reflected ED-track framing, not omitted methods. We will now define STBoost and
BLEEP at first mention, replace ``Ours,'' and move a concise
formulation/architecture into the main body.
\end{itemize}

\questionsep
\textbf{Q4 (Q3, L3).}\quad
\textit{What does cell alignment add beyond spot-centered prediction?}

\textbf{A.}\enspace We thank the reviewer for this suggestion.
\begin{itemize}
\item Across six native-Xenium samples, fixed-pipeline cell/8/16/55-\(\mu\)m
targets gave Gene Pearson values of 0.365, 0.365, 0.363, and 0.330. Yet, in two
representative samples, 55-\(\mu\)m pseudo-spots mixed cell types in
55.5--66.4\% of regions and affected 73.8--81.0\% of cells in dense tissue,
versus 3.5--4.1\% and 7.0--8.3\% in sparse tissue.
\item Thus, even when global correlation changes little, cell alignment
preserves cell identity and local heterogeneity needed for cell--cell
communication; links morphology, RNA, and perturbation response at the cell
unit for virtual-cell models; and permits tracing perturbation effects through
interacting cells across spatial tissue. These analyses are ill-posed when
heterogeneous cells are collapsed into one spot.
\end{itemize}
