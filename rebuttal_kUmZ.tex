\textbf{Q3 (P6, P7).}\quad
\textit{What are the benchmark inputs, experimental design, and STBoost
definitions?}

\textbf{A.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Input/output:} the input is histology alone and the output is the
RNA-expression vector of an aligned cell; no spot-level expression is supplied
at inference. Each training target is paired with a local cell crop and a
larger tissue-context crop.
\item \textbf{Protocols/metrics:} within-sample spatial holdout, cross-sample,
verified cross-patient, and cross-platform protocols test progressively harder
shifts using fixed targets and splits. We report gene-wise and cell-wise
Pearson/Spearman together with expression-detection F1.
\item \textbf{STBoost:} this framework lifts existing spot-level
image-to-expression methods to cell-level prediction by replacing their spot
interface with hierarchical cell/context images and cell-resolved targets
while retaining their prediction modules. Every BLEEP comparison in our tables
is therefore STBoosted BLEEP; STBoost-Ref is our image-only reference predictor.
\item Because this is a dataset paper, we originally placed the detailed
STBoost formulation, equations, and architecture figure in the Appendix. We
will define STBoost and BLEEP at first mention, remove the ambiguous label
``Ours,'' and move a concise architecture and formulation description into the
main body.
\end{itemize}

\questionsep
\textbf{Q4 (Q3, L3).}\quad
\textit{What does cell alignment add beyond spot-centered prediction?}

\textbf{A.}\enspace We thank the reviewer for this important suggestion.
\begin{itemize}
\item Across six native-Xenium samples, we held the pipeline fixed and changed
only target scale (cell/8/16/55 \(\mu\)m): Gene Pearson was
0.365/0.365/0.363/0.330, showing averaging can retain a similar global score.
\item In two representative samples, 55-\(\mu\)m pseudo-spots mixed cell types
in 55.5--66.4\% of regions and affected 73.8--81.0\% of cells in dense tissue,
versus 3.5--4.1\% and 7.0--8.3\% in sparse tissue.
\item Cell alignment reveals heterogeneity hidden by averaging and enables
cell-resolved studies such as cell--cell communication, virtual-cell
perturbation prediction, and tracing perturbation effects through interactions
across spatial tissue.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Q5 (P4, P8, P9, L4).}\quad
\textit{What do the context analysis and available metadata support?}

\textbf{A1 (Representativeness).}\enspace We agree with P9 that we should not
assume existing datasets are representative of ethnicity, sex/gender, or other
human traits. Ethnicity is undocumented in the upstream records, and other
context fields have variable coverage. We will report support and missingness,
never infer absent sensitive attributes, and avoid population-level fairness
claims unsupported by the data.

\textbf{A2 (Why context-bias evaluation matters).}\enspace We nevertheless
believe that context-aware audits are important for biomedical and pathology
foundation models: a strong average score can hide substantial failure in a
specific assay, dataset, acquisition site, age group, or disease context. We
tested this with patient-CV or leave-one-site-out evaluation and report
Average/Worst/Gap:

\textbf{A3 (Reporting scope).}
\begin{itemize}
\item The breadth benchmark uses 30 native-Xenium samples; HD transfer uses 16
source/target samples in eight pairs. Upstream donor/animal IDs are incomplete,
so these are sample-held-out unless donor independence is verified.
\item Because age is nested within sample/patient and gene panels differ,
AK/AD/AL supports a \emph{sample/age-confounded context shift}, not a causal
age effect. We will require multiple verified donors per subgroup comparison
and report per-organ sample records beside cell counts.
\end{itemize}
