\textbf{Opening response.}
We sincerely thank the reviewer for recognizing the potential value of sMMC and
for identifying the central question we had not answered clearly enough: what
research becomes possible because the resource is larger, cell aligned, and
context rich? This resource has accompanied me since my first PhD year, when I
was new to spatial transcriptomics. As I approach graduation, I hope its unified
release helps new students enter the field with fewer practical barriers.

We also clarify the new primary component. The current working manifest contains
99 unique samples (100 processing records) and 28,315,247 aligned targets. Our
in-house DBiC-seq collection contributes 21 samples and 54,304 measured cells;
paired-record QC removes 315, leaving 53,989 aligned cells. These records pair
cell morphology, RNA, and cellular context. A broader continuing DBiC-seq pool
now contains approximately 200,000 paired cells but is not counted in the table
below.

\textbf{Q1.}\quad
\textit{The extent to which this new data can truly promote relevant research
progress has not been fully explained.}

\textbf{A1 (Scale).}\enspace
We agree. The resource is valuable not because one predictor has solved
histology-to-expression inference, but because it makes previously difficult
evaluations measurable.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Objective:} test the value of additional training cells under a
fixed task.
\item \textbf{Design:} on native-Xenium sample HHDX011, we fixed the spatial
test regions, training-selected top-50 HVGs, and regression protocol while
varying training cells for seven frozen encoders:
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reproducibility:} the updated benchmark code, fixed splits,
configurations, and evaluation scripts for this table are now available in our
\href{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/sMMC-22M-DB75}{anonymous GitHub
repository}.
\item \textbf{Result:} image prediction exceeds coordinate and KNN controls in 28/30 samples. On
marker/HVG overlap, image Gene Pearson is 0.201 versus 0.031 for coordinates and
0.028 for KNN. Cell-type-stratified pseudobulk RMSE is 0.120 for image prediction
versus 0.224/0.187/0.188 for coordinates/KNN/training mean.
\item \textbf{Claim boundary:} because cell-type labels are expression derived,
this is aggregate biological calibration, not independent ground truth.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{A3 (Resolution).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Design/result:} on six native-Xenium samples, matched cell, 8-,
16-, and 55-\(\mu\)m supervision
gives Gene Pearson 0.365, 0.365, 0.363, and 0.330. In dense lung, 55.5\%--66.4\% of
55-\(\mu\)m pseudo-spots mix predicted cell types and contain 73.8\%--81.0\% of
evaluated cells, whereas sparse heart shows only 3.5\%--4.1\% mixed spots and
7.0\%--8.3\% affected cells.
\item \textbf{Significance:} cell-level evaluation exposes density-dependent
mixing hidden by coarse averaging; it is not claimed to be universally easier.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{A4 (Overall scientific value).}\enspace Together, these experiments turn
scale, resolution, and context from descriptive properties into testable
variables:
\begin{itemize}
\item data scaling and leakage-controlled breadth across organs;
\item organ/platform transfer and context-specific failure;
\item aggregation-induced oversmoothing and spatial leakage.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{A2 (Interpretation and protocol changes).}
\begin{itemize}
\item The ovarian comparison is age associated but sample/patient confounded,
not causal. Incomplete donor identity also means the 18-target result is
sample-held-out, not patient-held-out.
\item We will report organ-macro and platform-separated results rather than only
cell-weighted averages.
\item We will distinguish spatial in-domain, sample-held-out, and verified
patient-held-out protocols.
\item We will report Average/Worst/Gap with sample support and disclose
gene-panel overlap and metadata missingness.
\item We will label every field as directly measured, processed, generated, or
model output, and will not infer missing sensitive attributes.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{A3 (Significance).}\enspace These revisions clarify the scientific value:
sMMC does not remove
heterogeneity, but makes its downstream effects visible, measurable, and
reproducible across organs, platforms, spatial resolutions, and biological
contexts.
