Thus, genuinely new primary acquisition represents \(4/70=5.7\%\) of unique
source samples, \(9{,}806/23{,}953{,}632=0.041\%\) of measured/source units, and
\(9{,}806/20{,}255{,}975=0.048\%\) of aligned target cells. Conversely,
\(94.3\%\) of source samples and \(99.952\%\) of aligned target cells originate
from public data. The in-house pilot therefore adds three cell-line origins, not
new patient tissues or organ cohorts; in particular, 3T3 is a mouse-embryo-derived
fibroblast line, not brain tissue. We will revise the paper to state directly that
the resource's scale comes predominantly from public-data aggregation and
harmonization, while the genuinely new primary component is small.

For transparency, one HEST-overlapping breast slide occurs as two versioned
processing records, which is why the table distinguishes 70 unique samples from 71
records. Public Visium HD ``measured/source units'' are pre-aggregation
source/segmentation records; their aligned column reports canonical
2-\(\mu\)m bin-to-cell targets. Native Xenium cells are already cell resolved.

Beyond the QC-frozen pilot reported in the table, our in-house DBiC-seq
acquisition is continuing. The broader collection currently contains
approximately \(200{,}000\) paired cells that we can make available, with each
cell linked to matched morphology and RNA measurements and its corresponding
context information. This is an approximate release count rather than a
sample-level manifest count, so we do not silently fold it into the exact audited
totals above. If the broader in-house pool totals approximately \(200{,}000\)
cells, it would constitute roughly \(1\%\) of the expanded aligned-cell
inventory; we will report the exact sample count and fraction after the release
manifest and QC freeze are complete.

As a concrete exploratory use case, the four pilot samples comprise 10,000 raw
trap positions and 9,806 QC-paired cells. We used 2,100 morphology-QC C416 cells
to characterize a single-cell morphology state space and 2,497 QC HD701 cells
for out-of-fold prediction of an RNA ribosome module from image and geometry
features alongside a spatial control. This pilot illustrates how the paired
records can support analyses of relations among cell geometry and morphology,
RNA programs, and cell or cell-line identity. Extending the same design to
source-provided or marker-derived cell types is a benchmark use case, not a
result claimed by this pilot.

\questionsep
\textbf{7Gar-2.}\quad
\revcomment{Clear purpose and biological meaning of the per-axis experiments. For
each experiment, please lay out the benchmark objective, design, results, and
significance cleanly. In particular: What task and metric does Fig.\ 2 use? In the
resolution study, what exactly are STBoost, STBoost-Ref, and ``Ours''; what is
BLEEP; and what is ``STBoosted BLEEP''? For the age-effect analysis, what is the
precise claim, and why is it informative beyond the expected degradation under an
age shift?}

\response
We agree. Although the manuscript repeatedly emphasized scale, resolution, and
rich context, it did not state the purpose, design, result, and significance of
each experiment clearly enough. We will reorganize this section accordingly.

\textbf{Scale.}
\textbf{Objective:} test whether the increased data volume is useful---that is,
whether multiple pathology foundation models and image encoders improve when they
are given more training cells under the same prediction task.
\textbf{Design and metric:} Fig.\ 2 performs histology-image-to-gene-expression
prediction while holding the encoder, gene targets, preprocessing, and test set
fixed and increasing the fraction of available training cells from \(0.05\) to
\(0.25\). The primary metric is gene-wise Pearson correlation across test cells.
We use representative organ settings rather than all organs because this is a
controlled test of the value of additional data, not a breadth benchmark.
\textbf{Result and significance:} within the tested range, prediction performance
increases with the training-cell fraction across the evaluated encoders. This is
the intended conclusion of the experiment: pathology pretraining and additional
data improve prediction in these representative settings. We do not claim that
this establishes a universal scaling law or guarantees improvement for every
organ.
\needexp{Before submission, insert the audited per-ratio values, exact training-cell
counts, repetitions, and uncertainty for Fig.\ 2.}

\textbf{Resolution.}
\textbf{Objective:} test whether a spot-level image-to-expression pipeline can be
trained and evaluated on cell-aligned records and how it behaves under patient
shift. STBoost is the model-agnostic cell-aligned data interface; BLEEP is the
published spot-level baseline; STBoosted BLEEP is BLEEP retrained through this
interface; and STBoost-Ref is our native retrieval-based reference predictor.
``Ours'' in the current figure is STBoost-Ref and will be renamed. At inference,
STBoost-Ref receives only local and context histology crops, never query
expression. On the same 5,000 test cells and 18,085 genes, STBoost-Ref improves
most continuous metrics in \(A\!\to\!A\) and every reported metric in
\(A\!\to\!B\); for example, cross-patient F1 increases from \(0.0857\) to
\(0.1061\), and cell-wise Pearson from \(0.0896\) to \(0.2820\). This supports the
feasibility of the cell-aligned interface, not a claim that true single-cell
expression recovery is solved.

\textbf{Rich context.}
\textbf{Objective:} provide a showcase of why rich metadata is needed to audit
context-dependent performance bias. In standard machine-learning and computer-
vision tasks, a strong pooled score is insufficient if performance collapses for
a subgroup defined by age, sex, disease background, or another relevant context.
We argue that histology-to-expression benchmarks should take the same issue
seriously.
\textbf{Design and result:} within the ovarian-cancer cohort, we compare transfer
from AK (60+) to AD (60+) with transfer from AK (60+) to AL (40-). F1 changes from
\(0.289\) to \(0.072\), gene Spearman from \(0.036\) to \(0.006\), cell Spearman
from \(0.206\) to \(0.095\), and the predicted nonzero rate from \(0.166\) to
\(0.027\).
\textbf{Significance:} this large subgroup gap is the point of the showcase. It
shows that an average score can hide severe worst-context failure and motivates
reporting average performance, worst-context performance, the gap between them,
and subgroup support. The contribution of rich context is to make such audits and
future bias-mitigation studies possible; we do not claim to solve the bias here.
Because age is currently nested within sample/patient and tissue context and the
comparisons use unequal gene panels, we will describe this result as an
age-associated, sample-confounded shift rather than a causal age effect.

\questionsep
\textbf{7Gar-4.}\quad
\revcomment{The spot-to-cell component uses existing bin2cell-style methodology and
is not positioned against prior work.}

\response
We agree and will not present bin-to-cell aggregation as a new decomposition
algorithm. The dataset contribution is the provenance-preserving harmonization,
cell-centered multimodal interface, leakage-controlled split definitions, and
benchmarking across organs/platforms/context. The revision will explain the
spot/bin-to-cell construction before downstream experiments and expand related work
on Bin2Cell and other spot-to-cell or super-resolution methods. We will state that
derived Visium HD targets inherit the assumptions of those methods.
